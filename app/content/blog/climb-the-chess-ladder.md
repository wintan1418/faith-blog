The Chess Ladder is for the days when one puzzle isn't enough. It's a climb of ten chess puzzles, each harder than the one before, and the question is simple: how high can you get? This guide explains the rules and shares a few ways to climb higher.

## Where to find it

Open [/games/chess](/games/chess). The Puzzle of the Day is at the top. Scroll down to the section headed **♞ The ladder**, with the line "Climb as high as you can."

## The rules

The rules are printed on the page: "10 stages, each harder than the last. 3 tries per puzzle: a wrong move or a hint uses one. Run out and the climb starts again." Here's what that means in practice.

1. **Choose your level.** Tap **Easy**, **Medium** or **Hard**. Your climb starts at Stage 1 of 10.
2. **Solve the puzzle.** Read whose move it is and the themes above the board, then play.
3. **Guard your three tries.** Hearts under the board show how many you have left.
4. **A wrong move costs a try.** It's shown in red and taken back, with a message such as "Not the best move here. 2 tries left." Then you can think again.
5. **A hint costs a try too.** Use one only when you're stuck.
6. **Any checkmate counts.** If the puzzle asks for mate and you find a different checkmate from the expected one, it still counts.
7. **Solve it and tap Next.** You move up a stage.
8. **Run out of tries and the climb starts again** from Stage 1.

## Your best stage is remembered

When a climb ends, it isn't wasted. Brethreign keeps your best stage, so you always know the height to beat next time.

The ladder doesn't add to your leaderboard points. That's on purpose. It's a place to practise freely, take risks and learn, without worrying about your weekly score. Points come from the [Puzzle of the Day](/blog/chess-puzzle-of-the-day).

## Which level to choose

- **Easy** is for newer players and anyone warming up.
- **Medium** is for players who know the common patterns, such as forks, pins and simple mates.
- **Hard** is for experienced players who want a real test.

A good plan is to start on Easy. When you reach Stage 10 there, move up a level.

## How to climb higher

**Slow down on the first move.** Most lost tries come from playing the first move that looks good. Count every check, capture and threat before you touch a piece.

**Think about the reply.** After your move, what will your opponent do? Puzzles are built around a strong answer to the obvious move.

**Save your hints for the top stages.** A hint costs the same as a wrong move. On Stage 2 it's rarely worth it. On Stage 9 it might be.

**Learn from a lost climb.** When you run out of tries, look at the position again. The idea you missed will turn up in another puzzle.

<aside class="bl-tip"><b>Tip.</b> Climb with a friend. Play the same level, then share your best stage in a breath and see who gets higher this week.</aside>

## Patience pays

The ladder rewards patience more than speed. There's something in that for life too: steady steps, learning from the falls, and getting back up.
